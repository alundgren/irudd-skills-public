# One view doing three jobs

A recipe page on a cooking site.

## Before

One page holds the photo, the intro, the ingredient list, the steps, the
nutrition table, the notes, related recipes, and an edit form at the bottom
for the owner.

It is complete. Everything about this recipe is on it. That is the problem,
because "everything about this recipe" is not something anyone is trying to do.

Three different people are on that page. Someone deciding whether to cook it.
Someone standing at the stove with wet hands. Someone at the shop. All three
scroll past most of it.

## After

Three views, named after what people are doing.

- **Read.** Photo, intro, ingredients, steps. The decision view. It is the
  default because deciding comes first.
- **Cook.** Ingredients and steps only, larger type, one step at a time, screen
  kept awake. Nothing else on it.
- **Shop.** The ingredient list, scaled to the number of servings, each line
  checkable.

Edit moves to its own screen. It is used once per recipe by one person.

## Why

Principle 2, one view one job. Principle 1, name the task and show what the
task needs.

Note what did not happen. No content was deleted and no information design was
changed. The same material got split by goal instead of by "what we know about
this recipe".

## Carry this

If you cannot name the job a view does in a short sentence with a verb in it,
it is doing several. Split it by goal, not by data type.
